# MEAD case-study source notes

Working evidence map for the portfolio case study. This is not polished public copy.

## Sources reviewed

- Portfolio composition: Figma frame `79:2`, “MEAD — Desktop / V2 Deck-informed.”
- Product interface: MEAD final design file, High Fidelity Prototype page `1:2`.
- Presentation: `MEAD Presentation File.pdf`, 21 pages.

## Claims supported by the presentation

- MEAD means Meaningful Engagement in Advanced Dementia.
- The named student team had four members: Atharva Patil, Frida Montano, Lily Sivertson, and Mikaela Thompson.
- The project aimed to help caregivers assess engagement in people with advanced dementia who may have severe communication difficulties.
- The source workflow used Epicollect5 to collect short interaction videos for later expert analysis.
- The audit identified four experience problems: an unclear Add Entry action, no feedback after collection, manual upload and deletion, and a redundant form with unclear horizontal progress.
- The redesign walkthrough covered adding and verifying a care list, recording a new entry, and returning feedback.
- The presentation explicitly lists these limitations: no testing with actual caregivers, consent challenges, and privacy / UK GDPR requirements.
- Proposed next steps were a desktop experience, caregiver testing, the analysis algorithm, and refinement of feedback visualisation.

## Evidence boundaries used in the page

- Describe the result as a high-fidelity concept prototype, not a deployed care product or clinical assessment.
- Do not present the engagement values visible in the prototype as measured outcomes.
- Do not treat Amy as interviewed-user evidence; she was a scenario anchor used to walk through the design.
- Keep the analysis algorithm, privacy implementation, and consent management in the unresolved/future-work section.
- Frame feedback as a designed return loop that requires caregiver and specialist validation.
