# What Atharva is looking for, and how he works

## Role and level

Atharva is a recent HCI graduate with a computer science background, looking for
his first full-time role. He is targeting Product Designer, UX Designer, and
Design Engineer positions at entry or junior level.

The work he wants combines user research, interaction design, prototyping,
product thinking, and close collaboration with engineering. He is particularly
interested in roles where design and front-end implementation overlap, rather
than ones limited to visual execution.

The problems that interest him involve complex workflows, technical products,
AI-driven products, data-heavy interfaces, and cases where the designer has to
work out what information someone needs in order to make a decision. He wants to
keep moving toward work at the intersection of technology, product strategy, and
design.

## Location

He is searching across the United States and has considered remote, hybrid, and
onsite positions. He is open to relocating for the right opportunity, including
to San Francisco.

## Team and company fit

Atharva wants to work with experienced designers who will give him regular
feedback and critique his work. Mentorship matters to him because he is early in
his career, and he wants to contribute to real product work while learning from
people further along.

He values critique that stays close to the work without making disagreement
personal, a preference shaped by design school critique sessions. He prefers
teams where people can question decisions, explain their reasoning, and change
direction when the evidence says the original call was wrong.

He is comfortable with early-stage and ambiguous product work, having built his
own product while making the calls across research, design, implementation,
testing, and direction. He has agency experience through ByteOrange, working on
client-facing web projects, and has worked on consumer software, healthcare,
government workflows, employee training, and digital services.

## How he works

He starts by trying to understand the underlying user problem before deciding on
an interface. His research practice covers interviews, usability testing,
contextual inquiry, surveys, synthesis, journey mapping, information
architecture, and user flows.

He works closely with engineering where he can. His computer science background
means he is comfortable discussing implementation constraints, and he does not
treat handoff as the end of his involvement. In Figma he works across user flows,
wireframes, high-fidelity interfaces, interactive prototypes, responsive layouts,
components, variants, auto layout, variables, and developer handoff. He has used
Figma since 2021, and professionally since 2023.

He built Streaming Helper in React, TypeScript, and Supabase after first
designing and researching it as his graduate capstone. It includes a web
application and a Chrome extension. He has continued to develop it past the
capstone, working through authentication, notifications, friend discovery,
database decisions, product security, deployment, bugs, and user testing. When
priorities on it competed, he sought out perspectives from mentors and peers and
used those conversations to reach his own decision.

He does not treat having a rationale as proof that a design is correct. One
lesson from design school stuck: a decision can have a reasonable explanation and
still need to change if people keep struggling with it.

## Working with AI

Atharva uses AI throughout his design and development workflow, including
ChatGPT, Cursor, OpenAI Codex, Figma Make, and Claude. He uses it for research
synthesis, exploring product flows, prototyping, implementation, debugging,
understanding unfamiliar code, and reviewing changes.

He has run multiple AI agents in the same workflow, with one implementing a
change while another independently reviews or audits it. He still scopes the
work, reviews the result, tests it, and makes the final product and UX calls.

He has found that AI will produce technically valid work that makes the wrong
product assumption, or fixes a symptom rather than the underlying problem. He
accounts for that through review and testing.

## Strengths

- He works across research, UX and UI design, prototyping, and front-end
  implementation.
- His HCI and computer science backgrounds let him talk about both user
  experience and technical implementation.
- He has taken a product from problem framing and research through design,
  development, testing, and release. Streaming Helper is the main example.
- He has run research in complex domains. The U.S. Department of State project
  required his team to understand an existing operational workflow before
  proposing a new experience.
- He is comfortable with developer handoff, implementation discussions,
  stakeholder presentations, and client-facing work.
- He has designed responsive web interfaces and reusable UI components, and has
  worked with accessibility and WCAG.
- He is comfortable learning technical concepts a project demands. Streaming
  Helper pushed him from interface design into React, TypeScript, Supabase,
  database decisions, debugging, and deployment.
- He takes detailed critique well and revises on it.

## Where he is still developing

- He is early in his career and looking for his first full-time product design
  role.
- He has not built or extended a large-scale design system used across multiple
  product teams. His design-system work has been at smaller scale: reusable
  components, styles, variants, variables, typography, spacing, and responsive
  patterns in his own product and other design work.
- He is still learning from what happens after a product ships. With Streaming
  Helper he has been working on acquiring users and understanding retention and
  conversion, rather than stopping at prototype validation.
- He wants more experience inside established product teams, learning from
  experienced designers.
- He is continuing to build his front-end skills, and using them to work closer
  to implementation.

## What suits him less well

- Roles pitched at a seniority he has not reached. He would rather be accurate
  about his experience than oversell it.
- Environments where designers work without feedback or access to more
  experienced people.
- Environments where design decisions cannot be questioned or revised.
- Work confined to isolated visual deliverables, away from the product and its
  implementation.
- He is looking for full-time professional roles.
